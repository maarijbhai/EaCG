  __init__.py    Package doc; states that the classical pipeline is the

  ptbxl.py       PTB-XL loading and labels.
                 - load_metadata()      parses scp_codes, aggregates the five
                                        diagnostic superclasses
                 - superclass_matrix()  (N, 5) MULTI-LABEL indicator. Rows may
                                        sum to 0 or >1. Sigmoid + BCE, never
                                        softmax.
                 - split_folds()        strat_fold 1-8 / 9 / 10. Exists so no
                                        caller reaches for train_test_split;
                                        21,799 records come from 18,869
                                        patients and a random split leaks them.
                 - load_record()        (n, 12) float64 IN MILLIVOLTS.
                                        wfdb.rdsamp returns p_signal, which has
                                        already had ADC gain applied — anything
                                        that multiplies by gain again is
                                        double-converting. The function raises
                                        if a header does not declare mV.

  synth.py       Test signals and single-strip rendering.
                 - synthetic_ecg()      sum of five Gaussians. A FIXTURE, not a
                                        physiological model.
                 - render_printout()    one continuous lead, red grid, 1 mm
                                        minor / 5 mm major, 25 mm/s, 10 mm/mV
                 - draw_trace()         sub-pixel polyline (shift=4); integer
                                        vertices would quantise amplitude
                                        visibly at low px/mm
                 - RenderSpec           ground-truth geometry. Explicitly the
                                        answer key: digitise() must never be
                                        handed one, or the calibration question
                                        is begged.

  layout.py      Clinical 3x4 + rhythm strip.
                 - render_12lead()      12 cells of 2.5 s + full-width lead II
                 - CellSpec             records t_start_s/t_end_s IN THE
                                        ORIGINAL RECORDING per cell, because
                                        columns are CONSECUTIVE windows — V4
                                        (column 3) is 7.5-10.0 s while lead I
                                        (column 0) is 0.0-2.5 s. They are not
                                        simultaneous.
                 - printed_fraction     measured at 33.3%, matching the
                                        documented invariant exactly
                 - lead labels are drawn in the same ink as the trace, so
                                        binarisation returns them as signal.
                                        Deliberate: real printouts are labelled
                                        and segmentation must reject them.

  digitise.py    The classical pipeline. Single continuous strip only.
                 - estimate_grid_pitch()  see section 4
                 - binarise_trace()       thresholds 255-R. Both white paper
                                          and red grid keep a saturated red
                                          channel; neutral ink does not. Breaks
                                          on black-and-white photocopies, as
                                          documented.
                 - trace_positions()      per-column ink centroid; NaN where no
                                          ink, so "no data" cannot be read as
                                          "0 mV"
                 - baseline_from_trace()  median. See section 4.
                 - pixels_to_mv()         (baseline - y) / px_per_mv. See
                                          section 4.
                 - digitise()             returns DigitiseResult with the
                                          signal, the recovered calibration and
                                          a coverage figure

  evaluate.py    Metrics, reported together or not at all.
                 - snr_db, shifted_snr_db, rmse_mv, pcc
                 - gain_ratio()   ADDED beyond the CLAUDE.md list. Least-squares
                                  amplitude scale. It is what separates "the
                                  calibration is wrong" from "the image needs
                                  more pixels" — both raise RMSE, and without
                                  it the two are indistinguishable. This turned
                                  out to matter; see section 5.
                 - Metrics.diagnose()  reads the metrics jointly and names the
                                  likely fault in words

  run_baseline.py   synthetic signal -> render -> digitise -> score, swept over

  run_ptbxl.py      the same on real PTB-XL waveforms, test fold only. Can also

4. THE THREE THINGS MOST LIKELY TO BE GOT WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1  SIGN CONVENTION.  Image rows grow downward; millivolts grow upward.

       mv = (baseline_px - y_px) / px_per_mv

     Writing (y_px - baseline_px) yields a waveform correct in every respect
     except inverted — which PCC reports as a tidy -1.0 rather than as a
     failure. Verified both ways: upright PCC +0.995, deliberately flipped
     PCC -0.995, and diagnose() names the sign convention in the flipped case.

4.2  GRID PITCH, and why it no longer locks onto a harmonic.  The pitch is
     taken as the MEDIAN SPACING BETWEEN SUCCESSIVE AUTOCORRELATION PEAKS,
     not the tallest peak. On ECG paper the 5 mm peak is taller than the 1 mm
     one — at a 5 mm lag the heavy major rules align with each other, whereas
     at 1 mm the major rules must align with minor ones. Taking the global
     maximum therefore locks onto the 5x harmonic. Peak spacing is immune to
     which harmonic is tallest, because the peaks at 1, 2, 3 and 4 mm all
     still exist.

     Measured: pitch recovered to within 0.01% at 4, 6, 8, 12 and 16 px/mm.
     At 3 px/mm it returns NaN and digitise() raises, rather than returning a
     confident wrong answer.

     Where it would still fail, and this is not hypothetical:
       - Below ~3-4 px/mm, or under blur or JPEG chroma subsampling, the 1 mm
         rules stop resolving and only the 5 mm ones survive. The peak spacing
         is then a clean 5 mm and the function returns a CONFIDENT 5x ERROR.
         Nothing in the autocorrelation distinguishes this. Guard it outside
         by sanity-checking the implied paper speed.
       - Rotation beyond ~1 degree smears line energy across the projection.
       - Perspective makes a single pitch wrong everywhere but the centre;
         GridEstimate.anisotropy catches the gross cases.
       - Non-red grids: redness() returns near-zero on a photocopy.

4.3  BASELINE ESTIMATION is the median inked row, and this is a real
     limitation rather than a settled choice. The median assumes the trace
     spends more than half its columns near baseline. That fails for a lead
     with sustained large deflections — deep S waves in V1-V2, giant T waves,
     VT, marked ST elevation at high rate. The estimate then drifts toward
     the deflection and the whole signal acquires a DC offset which is
     INVISIBLE TO PCC (offset-invariant) and shows up only in RMSE. This is
     precisely why the metrics have to be read together.

4.4  RESAMPLING BIAS in align_length — the default was changed to
     "truncate" because "resample" flatters the digitiser. Stretching the
     reconstruction onto the reference's length is a time warp fitted from
     the very quantity being measured: if grid pitch was 2% off, so px_per_s
     was 2% off, resampling by length ratio silently UNDOES that error before
     any metric sees it.

     Measured, on a signal with a deliberate 2% time error:

       truncate   SNR  -2.08 dB     (correctly terrible)
       resample   SNR  +49.40 dB    (error erased)

     A 51 dB swing on the same pair of signals. "resample" is still
     available, for the case where the length difference is known to be a
     cropping artefact rather than a calibration one.

5.2  THE MOST IMPORTANT RESULT HERE is the gain column. The classical tracer
     SYSTEMATICALLY UNDER-READS AMPLITUDE — by 40% at 4 px/mm, and still by
     4.4% at 16 px/mm — while grid pitch is recovered essentially perfectly.

     The cause is column-centroid tracing. On a steep QRS upstroke a single
     pixel column contains a tall vertical run of ink, and its centroid sits
     at the run's midpoint, so the R peak is clipped by roughly half the
     within-column excursion. It is resolution-dependent, it is not a
     calibration fault, and it is the dominant amplitude error of the
     classical path. It is also the obvious thing a learned tracer would be
     expected to beat, which makes it a useful number for the thesis.

     Without gain_ratio this shows up only as elevated RMSE and would
     plausibly have been misread as a calibration bug for some time.

9. B5 FIXED — the renderer no longer clips in silence
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

9.1  THE DEFECT. synth.render_printout defaulted to height_mm=40, which at
     10 mm/mV is +/-2.0 mV. cv2.polylines clips at the canvas edge without
     raising, so an over-tall trace flattened against the boundary rather
     than vanishing. Coverage still read ~100% and nothing warned.

     Why it mattered more than an ordinary rendering bug: the project's
     headline result is that the classical tracer under-reads amplitude
     (gain ratio 0.87 on real records). Canvas clipping pushes gain in the
     SAME direction, so the two were confounded.

9.2  HOW MUCH OF PTB-XL DOES NOT FIT. Surveyed over the whole test fold —
     2,198 records, 100 Hz, all 12 leads, 26,376 lead-traces. Half-extent
     required is max(|min|, |max|), because an ECG is not centred on zero.

       lead-traces needing more than +/-2.0 mV   1,971 / 26,376    7.5%
       records with at least one such lead         892 /  2,198   40.6%

       worst leads   V4 20.2%   V2 17.7%   V5 16.3%   V3 16.1%   V6 7.3%
       best leads    AVR 0.1%   I 0.5%   AVL 1.0%   II 1.4%

       per-lead half-extent   p50 0.93   p99 3.28   max 12.97 mV
       worst lead per record  p50 1.84   p99 5.25   max 12.97 mV

     Asymmetry is real and large: |min| exceeds |max| on 94.7% of AVR
     traces and 93.2% of V1. Sizing a canvas from signal.max() alone would
     under-size precisely the leads that overflow most.

9.3  THE CHANGE. synth.py only.

     - CLEARANCE_MM = 2.0 and DEFAULT_HEIGHT_MM = 70.0 added.
     - required_height_mm(signal, mm_per_mv, clearance_mm) added — the fit
       computation, from actual min and max.
     - render_printout gains on_overflow="raise"|"expand"|"clip", default
       "raise", and clearance_mm. Its height_mm default moves 40 -> 70.
     - RenderSpec gains v_min_mv, v_max_mv, required_height_mm and clipped,
       plus height_mm and headroom_mm properties. All four new fields have
       defaults, so layout.py's hand-built RenderSpec is unaffected.

     WHY 70 mm, i.e. the 99th percentile. Height costs blank paper and
     nothing else — it does not change px_per_mv, so amplitude resolution is
     untouched and only the pixel count grows. But the tail is brutal: p100
     would need 263 mm for a handful of records. 70 mm covers 99% of
     lead-traces, and because the default is now "raise" the residual 1%
     leaves the sample loudly and countably rather than quietly biased.
     Measured attrition on V2, the second-worst lead: 1 skip in 40 records.

9.7  A SECOND CONFOUND IN THE RESOLUTION SWEEP — REPORTED, NOT FIXED.
     The task asked whether trace thickness scales with resolution. IT DOES
     NOT. trace_thickness is a fixed 2 px at every px_per_mm, so the ink is
     a different physical width at every point in the sweep:

       4 px/mm -> 2 px = 0.500 mm        16 px/mm -> 2 px = 0.125 mm

     A 4x physical variation across the sweep. And ink width is the most
     influential parameter in the entire experiment. At a FIXED 8 px/mm,
     varying only thickness:

       thickness    1p     2p     3p     4p     5p     6p
       = mm      0.125  0.250  0.375  0.500  0.625  0.750
       gain        nan  0.864  0.725  0.726  0.540  0.541
       SNR dB      nan  13.81   7.32   7.32   3.19   3.16
       coverage   3.4%   100%   100%   100%   100%   100%

     A swing of 0.32 in gain from ink width alone — more than twice the
     whole amplitude deficit under study. (1 px collapses entirely: the
     anti-aliased line is too faint for Otsu, coverage 3.4%, another B2
     NaN.)

     Holding ink at a physically constant 0.3 mm instead, the sweep's
     trend REVERSES above 8 px/mm:

       px/mm            4.0    6.0    8.0   12.0   16.0
       gain @ 2 px     0.598  0.793  0.864  0.928  0.956
       gain @ 0.3 mm     nan  0.793  0.864  0.838  0.759

     So the clean monotone "more pixels per millimetre is better" in 9.6 is
     at least partly an artefact of the line getting physically thinner as
     resolution rises. A defensible resolution sweep must hold physical ink
     width constant.

     Related, and also unfixed: draw_grid writes 1-pixel rules at every
     resolution, so the grid has no physical width either (0.250 mm at
     4 px/mm, 0.062 mm at 16). Real ECG paper rules are ~0.1-0.2 mm.
     render_printout has no notion of physical line width at all —
     everything but the geometry is specified in pixels.

     NOT FIXED because it changes the meaning of every number in the
     resolution sweep, and that is a decision to take deliberately rather
     than as a side effect of a clipping fix.

10.1 WHAT EXISTS NOW. Three new files, none of which modify anything that
     already existed:

       python/make_print_target.py   PTB-XL record -> print-ready PDF at true
                                     physical scale, + .npy ground truth
                                     + JSON manifest
       python/score_photo.py         photograph + manifest -> per-stage report
                                     and the full metric set
       docs/photo_protocol.md        what a human must do, precisely enough
                                     to repeat

     digitise.py was NOT modified. score_photo.py calls
     estimate_grid_pitch, binarise_trace, trace_positions and digitise as
     they are, and passes the grid estimate it already computed into
     digitise(grid=...) so the pitch it reports is exactly the pitch used.

10.2 TRUE PHYSICAL SCALE, and how it is achieved. px_per_mm = dpi / 25.4
     (11.811 at the 300 dpi default). The page is composed at that scale and
     written through Pillow with an explicit resolution, so the PDF MediaBox
     is the true physical size. Verified by parsing the MediaBox back out:

       841.92 x 595.20 pt  =  297.011 x 209.973 mm
       A4 landscape        =  297.000 x 210.000 mm
       error               =  +0.011 / -0.027 mm   (0.004% / 0.013%)

     That residual is whole-pixel rounding of the page canvas and is three
     orders of magnitude below the few-percent errors that "Fit to page"
     introduces. The strip's own size is not rounded in the manifest: it is
     recovered back from the pixel dimensions actually produced
     (260.01 x 70.02 mm), and that recovered value is the ground truth
     scoring uses.

     DPI is used at print time, where a pixel really is 1/dpi inch, and then
     thrown away. Nothing downstream infers scale from image DPI — Reyna et
     al. are explicit that the correspondence does not hold for camera
     capture, which is the whole reason the grid has to be the ruler.

10.3 WHAT IS ON THE PAGE, and why in that arrangement.

       header text         record identity, calibration, dpi, git commit,
                           and the instruction to print at 100%
       THE STRIP           the gridded region -- the only thing to crop to
       crop marks          L-shaped, at the four strip corners, pointing
                           AWAY from the strip
       ruler band          100 mm horizontal, 50 mm vertical, 10 mm = 1 mV

     Everything except the trace sits outside the strip deliberately.
     digitise traces every column it is given and cannot reject non-trace
     ink, so a calibration pulse or a label inside the strip would be
     digitised as signal. The crop marks' inner vertex sits exactly on the
     strip corner without putting ink inside it — "the corner of the grid"
     is a judgement call at photograph resolution, a printed vertex is not.

     The first draft had the 50 mm bar centred on the ruler band, which put
     it INSIDE the strip. Caught on inspection and fixed; the band now hangs
     downward from below the crop marks.

     Three references rather than one, because they fail differently: the
     100 mm bar catches uniform scaling ("Fit to page", the likeliest
     fault), the 50 mm vertical catches ANISOTROPIC scaling that a
     horizontal check alone would miss, and the 10 mm = 1 mV bar states the
     same ruler in the unit the result is reported in.

10.4 RECTIFICATION IS MANUAL AND DELIBERATELY SO. Four corners are supplied
     (--corners, --pick, or corners_px in the manifest), not detected.
     Automatic page detection and deskew are a later pipeline stage; building
     them here would confound the measurement with the quality of a detector
     nobody has written. This is the manual stand-in, not the real thing.

     The warp output size comes ONLY from the photograph's own corner
     geometry — the mean length of each pair of opposite edges. It never
     uses the strip's known aspect ratio. Forcing the true aspect would hand
     the estimator half the answer it is being tested on and would erase the
     anisotropy that perspective introduces, which is one of the things
     worth measuring.

     True px/mm for scoring = warp output size / the strip's known physical
     size. Ground truth is used to SCORE the estimate, never to produce it.

10.5 TOOLING SELF-TEST ON SIMULATED PHOTOGRAPHS. No print has been made and
     no photograph taken. To prove the two scripts run end to end before a
     human spends time printing, the rendered page was put through a
     synthetic camera: perspective warp, 2.5 degrees of rotation, lens blur,
     illumination gradient, sensor noise, JPEG 88, at several frame sizes.

     THESE ARE NOT PHOTOGRAPHS. No ink bleed, no paper texture, no fibre, no
     thermal fade, no specular highlight, no rolling shutter, no chromatic
     aberration, and the corner coordinates are exact rather than clicked.
     The simulation is strictly easier than reality and every number from it
     is optimistic. It proves the pipeline runs; it measures nothing about
     the field.

     A 12 MP simulated capture, square-ish on, scored:

       rectified        2893 x 786 px from a 4032 x 3024 frame
       true px/mm       11.126 horizontal, 11.226 vertical
       estimated        11.127 (+0.01%),  11.264 (+0.34%)
       SNR 8.48 dB   RMSE 0.0429 mV   PCC 0.994   gain 0.954
       best shift       +0 samples

10.6 THE SELF-TEST FOUND SOMETHING REAL: A SILENT 5x HARMONIC LOCK.

     At an 8 MP frame, the COMBINATION of lens blur, JPEG compression and an
     uneven illumination gradient made estimate_grid_pitch lock onto the
     5 mm major rules on the VERTICAL axis only:

       condition                        est x    est y   lock   PCC    gain
       warp only                         9.00     9.07     -    0.995  0.962
       + blur                            9.00     9.08     -    0.993  0.950
       + blur + jpeg88                   9.00     9.09     -    0.994  0.950
       + blur + jpeg88 + light           9.00    36.37    5x    0.994  0.238
       + blur + jpeg88 + light + noise   9.00    36.38    5x    0.989  0.237
       + light only                      9.00     9.08     -    0.995  0.964
       + noise only                      9.00     9.07     -    0.995  0.961

     (true px/mm 9.01 both axes; a separate sweep from 18.8 MP down to
     3.1 MP with blur and JPEG but NO lighting gradient never locked, so
     this is not simply a resolution floor.)

     Read the last two columns of the locked rows. PCC 0.994 — the shape is
     recovered essentially perfectly. Gain 0.238 — every amplitude is wrong
     by a factor of five. Nothing raised. digitise accepted the pitch
     because it is finite and positive.

     This is audit finding B1 reproduced in practice, and it is the exact
     failure evaluate.py's module docstring warns about: high PCC with a
     wrong gain is a CALIBRATION fault, and a report quoting PCC alone would
     have called this an excellent reconstruction.

     The guard already exists and is already ignored. In the locked case
     anisotropy = |9.00 - 36.37| / 22.69 = 1.21, against 0.012 in the clean
     case — a hundredfold difference that GridEstimate computes and digitise
     never reads. Fixing B1 is now backed by a measured case rather than an
     argument. NOT fixed here: B1 was explicitly out of scope.

10.7 A NEW FINDING, REPORTED NOT FIXED — Metrics.diagnose() misattributes.
     In one locked run (x on 2x, y on 5x) PCC came out at -0.069, i.e.
     essentially uncorrelated, and diagnose() reported:

       "inverted: shape matches but sign is flipped -- check the
        pixels-to-mV sign convention"

     The sign convention was fine. diagnose() branches on `pcc < 0.0`, so
     any trivially negative correlation is reported as a sign inversion and
     sends the reader to debug the one thing that is not wrong. Claiming
     inversion should require a strongly negative PCC (say < -0.7); between
     roughly -0.7 and +0.7 the honest answer is that the tracer lost the
     waveform. Call this B9. Not fixed: evaluate.py is outside this task.

10.8 OBSERVATION: mild blur HELPS the centroid tracer. At 12 MP the clean
     warp scored PCC 0.875 / gain 0.846, and adding lens blur and JPEG
     improved it to 0.986 / 0.946. A hard-edged resampled line gives Otsu a
     ragged mask; a slightly blurred one binarises to a smoother, more
     symmetric run whose centroid is steadier. Worth remembering before
     anyone "improves" capture by sharpening.

10.9 WHAT A HUMAN MUST DO BEFORE ANY REAL NUMBER EXISTS. In order, and none
     of it is optional:

       1. Run make_print_target.py. Keep the .pdf, _truth.npy and
          _manifest.json together — the .npy is what the photograph is
          scored against and without it the photograph is unmeasurable.
       2. Print at 100% scale, in COLOUR, on plain matte A4, landscape.
          Turn OFF "Fit to page" / "Shrink oversized pages" — they are on by
          default in most print dialogs. Greyscale breaks binarise_trace
          outright: the grid becomes neutral and is returned AS the trace.
       3. Measure the printed 100 mm and 50 mm bars with a real ruler.
          Tolerance +/-0.5 mm. If either is wrong, reprint — do not
          photograph it and do not try to correct afterwards. A scaled print
          is a ruler of unknown length and nothing in the image reveals the
          factor.
       4. Photograph per docs/photo_protocol.md: flat matte surface, EVEN
          DIFFUSE LIGHT (see 10.6 — uneven light is what broke the grid
          estimate in simulation), highest resolution, HDR off, flash off,
          focus on the grid. Nine shots: three identical square-on repeats
          first, then two rotations, two tilts, one deliberately lit from
          one side, one dim handheld.
       5. Transfer at full resolution. Not through WhatsApp, Messenger,
          Slack or iMessage — all recompress and several downscale.
       6. Run score_photo.py --pick --save-corners --diagnostics, and LOOK
          at the overlay image before believing any number.

     Until step 4 happens there is no real-image measurement and this
     changelog should not be read as containing one.

9.1 DESKEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Method: projection profile sharpness maximisation. The grid is a periodic
lattice of straight lines; axis-aligned, its projection onto an image axis
is a comb of sharp peaks, and rotation smears them together. Sharpness of
the projection is therefore a function of angle with a maximum at the true
skew.

The search never warps the image. Binning pixels by y - x*tan(a) collects
any line of slope a into one bin, and because rounding that index makes the
shift constant over runs of adjacent columns, each projection is one
grouped column sum plus a few hundred shifted vector adds. No interpolation
happens during the search, which matters because interpolation is a
low-pass filter acting on exactly the detail being measured. At a = 0 the
operation is identical to the projection estimate_grid_pitch already takes.

Coarse pass: +/-10 deg at 0.5 deg on a copy reduced to 900 px longest side.
Fine pass: +/-0.75 deg at 0.05 deg at full resolution, then a parabolic fit
to the objective for sub-step precision.

RESULTS (results/deskew/):

  Angle recovery, 3 fixtures x 17 true angles over +/-8 deg:
    max abs error 0.0043 deg, median 0.00000, mean 0.00034.
    Worst cases are all on the 150 dpi fixture, the lowest-resolution one.

  End-to-end, 12 PTB-XL test-fold records, lead II, 8 px/mm strips:
    unrotated            SNR 8.58  RMSE 0.0494  PCC 0.973  gain 0.869
    rotated 1 deg        12/12 FAIL (grid pitch NaN)
    deskewed 1 deg       SNR 8.60  RMSE 0.0503  PCC 0.963  gain 0.857
    round-trip control   SNR 8.49  RMSE 0.0503  PCC 0.963  gain 0.857
    rotated 3 deg        12/12 FAIL
    deskewed 3 deg       SNR 8.35  RMSE 0.0493  PCC 0.970  gain 0.864
    round-trip control   SNR 8.36  RMSE 0.0493  PCC 0.970  gain 0.864

    Deskew matches the round-trip control to three decimals. The control
    rotates and unrotates by the *known* angle, so it carries the
    interpolation cost and none of the estimation error. The two agreeing
    means the angle estimate contributes nothing measurable, and the whole
    residual gap to the unrotated condition -- about 1 point of PCC and 1.2
    of gain -- is the price of one interpolation.

  Failure boundaries, all at 2 deg unless stated:
    angle          exact to +/-44 deg; aliases by 90 deg at 46 deg, which
                   is the square lattice symmetry itself, not a defect
    blur           exact to sigma = 8 px (margin falls 569 -> 33)
    JPEG           exact to quality 5 (margin falls 464 -> 52)
    grid fade      exact down to 10% saturation; FAILS at 0% (pure grey)
                   with an explicit error, and a luminance response_fn
                   recovers the same 2.000 deg with margin 307 -- the
                   method is colour-agnostic, only the default front end
                   is red-specific
    grid coverage  exact to 15% of frame (error 0.0018 deg), but the
                   margin collapses 337 -> 12.4, so margin is the signal
                   to watch

  Cost. estimate_skew against estimate_grid_pitch, median of 3:
    2.10 MP    195 / 347 ms   vs  29 / 32 ms     x6.8 - x11.0
    3.74 MP    372 / 540 ms   vs  50 / 55 ms     x7.4 -  x9.9
    8.41 MP   1691 / 1941 ms  vs 130 / 139 ms   x13.0 - x14.0
    (first figure at 0 deg, second at 3 deg; cost grows with |angle|
    because the shear spans more bins.)

    This is the headline problem. Deskew is 7-14x the cost of what was
    already the most expensive stage, and it is superlinear in pixels.
    On a phone this does not fit as written. The coarse pass is cheap; the
    fine pass at full resolution is where the time goes, and reducing its
    resolution is the obvious lever. Not attempted yet.

  Sharpness measure. variance and ssd both reach ~0.001 deg accuracy;
  ssd has 4.6x the median peak prominence (660 vs 143) at equal cost, so
  ssd is the default. kurtosis fails outright (median error 3.9 deg).
  entropy is accurate but its margin is not interpretable, being a ratio
  of two negative numbers.

  Interpolation, simulation held fixed at Lanczos:
    lanczos   pitch shift 0.064%  contrast 0.919  PCC 0.9697  gain 0.8779
    nearest               0.070%           0.949      0.9628      0.8727
    bicubic               0.075%           0.949      0.9592      0.8693
    bilinear              0.090%           0.926      0.9537      0.8647

    Lanczos is the default on the waveform metrics. The robust finding is
    negative: bilinear, the OpenCV default, is worst on every axis.

9.2 A MISTAKE WORTH RECORDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The first interpolation comparison varied the warp used to *simulate* the
test rotation together with the warp used to *correct* it. It concluded
nearest-neighbour won on every axis by a wide margin, and that conclusion
was written into the module as its default before the flaw was noticed. An
image simulated with nearest arrives at the correction step less blurred,
so nearest was being credited for its own simulation. With the simulation
pinned to Lanczos the ordering changes and Lanczos wins.

run_deskew.py now has separate --interp and --sim-interp flags. CLAUDE.md
gained a convention line about it.

9.3 B1 GUARDS, AND WHAT THEY CANNOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

GridEstimate.confidence and .anisotropy were documented as the way to
distrust a pitch estimate and were consulted by nothing. digitise.py was
modified to close that, which is the whole of what was changed there:

  - GridEstimate gains spacing_cv (default NaN, so existing construction
    sites keep working); _pitch_1d returns a fourth value to supply it.
  - New CalibrationCheck dataclass and check_calibration function.
  - digitise() gains expected_duration_s and strict (default True) and
    calls the check; DigitiseResult gains .calibration.

Guards: an axis that failed to resolve, anisotropy above 0.15, and implied
paper speed outside 15-60 mm/s. spacing_cv is advisory only.

The measurements say plainly what does and does not work.

confidence does not discriminate. It sat at 0.68 on an estimate that was
399% wrong and 0.63 on one that was correct. The reason is structural:
peaks are selected as a fraction of the maximum, so the survivors are tall
by construction and their mean height is close to a constant of the
algorithm.

spacing_cv does not discriminate either, in the way that matters:
    render at 4.0 px/mm    pitch correct to -0.0%    spacing_cv 0.412
    fixture at 200 dpi     pitch wrong by +399%      spacing_cv 0.184
It is high on a good estimate at low resolution (quantisation) and low on
a bad one (an unresolved minor grid is a genuinely regular comb of 5 mm
rules). A major/minor autocorrelation height ratio was also tried and
fails the same way: 1.21 and 1.31 on correct estimates, 1.48 on one that
was 4x wrong. Hence advisory, not a rejection criterion.

The paper-speed check is the one that works, and it works because it
brings in a fact from outside the image:
    fixture 200 dpi clean   implied 5.6 mm/s   REJECTED  (true err +399%)
    fixture blurred/JPEG    implied 5.6 mm/s   REJECTED
    render at 8.0 px/mm     implied 26.0 mm/s  accepted  (true err -0.0%)
    render at 4.0 px/mm     implied 26.0 mm/s  accepted  (true err -0.0%)
    render at 2.5 px/mm     implied  6.5 mm/s  REJECTED  (true err +300%)
No false positives and no false negatives across the eleven conditions
tested. It is skipped, with a warning saying so, when the caller supplies
no expected_duration_s -- which is most callers today.

This confirms what the estimate_grid_pitch docstring already predicted:
nothing inside the autocorrelation can distinguish a 1 mm grid from a 5 mm
grid misread as 1 mm, because they are the same picture at different
distances.
