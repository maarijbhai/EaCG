Written to the module layout in CLAUDE.md. British spelling throughout

  __init__.py    Package doc; states that the classical pipeline is the
                 control condition and is not to be improved away.

  ptbxl.py       PTB-XL loading and labels.
                 - load_metadata()      parses scp_codes, aggregates the five
                                        diagnostic superclasses
                 - superclass_matrix()  (N, 5) MULTI-LABEL indicator. Rows may
                                        sum to 0 or >1. Sigmoid + BCE, never
                                        softmax.
                 - split_folds()        strat_fold 1-8 / 9 / 10. Exists so no
                                        caller reaches for train_test_split;
                                        21,799 records come from 18,869
                                        patients and a random split leaks them.
                 - load_record()        (n, 12) float64 IN MILLIVOLTS.
                                        wfdb.rdsamp returns p_signal, which has
                                        already had ADC gain applied — anything
                                        that multiplies by gain again is
                                        double-converting. The function raises
                                        if a header does not declare mV.

  synth.py       Test signals and single-strip rendering.
                 - synthetic_ecg()      sum of five Gaussians. A FIXTURE, not a
                                        physiological model.
                 - render_printout()    one continuous lead, red grid, 1 mm
                                        minor / 5 mm major, 25 mm/s, 10 mm/mV
                 - draw_trace()         sub-pixel polyline (shift=4); integer
                                        vertices would quantise amplitude
                                        visibly at low px/mm
                 - RenderSpec           ground-truth geometry. Explicitly the
                                        answer key: digitise() must never be
                                        handed one, or the calibration question
                                        is begged.

  layout.py      Clinical 3x4 + rhythm strip.
                 - render_12lead()      12 cells of 2.5 s + full-width lead II
                 - CellSpec             records t_start_s/t_end_s IN THE
                                        ORIGINAL RECORDING per cell, because
                                        columns are CONSECUTIVE windows — V4
                                        (column 3) is 7.5-10.0 s while lead I
                                        (column 0) is 0.0-2.5 s. They are not
                                        simultaneous.
                 - printed_fraction     measured at 33.3%, matching the
                                        documented invariant exactly
                 - lead labels are drawn in the same ink as the trace, so
                                        binarisation returns them as signal.
                                        Deliberate: real printouts are labelled
                                        and segmentation must reject them.

  digitise.py    The classical pipeline. Single continuous strip only.
                 - estimate_grid_pitch()  see section 4
                 - binarise_trace()       thresholds 255-R. Both white paper
                                          and red grid keep a saturated red
                                          channel; neutral ink does not. Breaks
                                          on black-and-white photocopies, as
                                          documented.
                 - trace_positions()      per-column ink centroid; NaN where no
                                          ink, so "no data" cannot be read as
                                          "0 mV"
                 - baseline_from_trace()  median. See section 4.
                 - pixels_to_mv()         (baseline - y) / px_per_mv. See
                                          section 4.
                 - digitise()             returns DigitiseResult with the
                                          signal, the recovered calibration and
                                          a coverage figure

  evaluate.py    Metrics, reported together or not at all.
                 - snr_db, shifted_snr_db, rmse_mv, pcc
                 - gain_ratio()   ADDED beyond the CLAUDE.md list. Least-squares
                                  amplitude scale. It is what separates "the
                                  calibration is wrong" from "the image needs
                                  more pixels" — both raise RMSE, and without
                                  it the two are indistinguishable. This turned
                                  out to matter; see section 5.
                 - Metrics.diagnose()  reads the metrics jointly and names the
                                  likely fault in words

  run_baseline.py   synthetic signal -> render -> digitise -> score, swept over

  run_ptbxl.py      the same on real PTB-XL waveforms, test fold only. Can also

  verify_ptbxl.py   checksums the local copy against the shipped SHA256SUMS.txt.

4. THE THREE THINGS MOST LIKELY TO BE GOT WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1  SIGN CONVENTION.  Image rows grow downward; millivolts grow upward.

       mv = (baseline_px - y_px) / px_per_mv

     Writing (y_px - baseline_px) yields a waveform correct in every respect
     except inverted — which PCC reports as a tidy -1.0 rather than as a
     failure. Verified both ways: upright PCC +0.995, deliberately flipped
     PCC -0.995, and diagnose() names the sign convention in the flipped case.

4.2  GRID PITCH, and why it no longer locks onto a harmonic.  The pitch is
     taken as the MEDIAN SPACING BETWEEN SUCCESSIVE AUTOCORRELATION PEAKS,
     not the tallest peak. On ECG paper the 5 mm peak is taller than the 1 mm
     one — at a 5 mm lag the heavy major rules align with each other, whereas
     at 1 mm the major rules must align with minor ones. Taking the global
     maximum therefore locks onto the 5x harmonic. Peak spacing is immune to
     which harmonic is tallest, because the peaks at 1, 2, 3 and 4 mm all
     still exist.

     Measured: pitch recovered to within 0.01% at 4, 6, 8, 12 and 16 px/mm.
     At 3 px/mm it returns NaN and digitise() raises, rather than returning a
     confident wrong answer.

     Where it would still fail, and this is not hypothetical:
       - Below ~3-4 px/mm, or under blur or JPEG chroma subsampling, the 1 mm
         rules stop resolving and only the 5 mm ones survive. The peak spacing
         is then a clean 5 mm and the function returns a CONFIDENT 5x ERROR.
         Nothing in the autocorrelation distinguishes this. Guard it outside
         by sanity-checking the implied paper speed.
       - Rotation beyond ~1 degree smears line energy across the projection.
       - Perspective makes a single pitch wrong everywhere but the centre;
         GridEstimate.anisotropy catches the gross cases.
       - Non-red grids: redness() returns near-zero on a photocopy.

4.3  BASELINE ESTIMATION is the median inked row, and this is a real
     limitation rather than a settled choice. The median assumes the trace
     spends more than half its columns near baseline. That fails for a lead
     with sustained large deflections — deep S waves in V1-V2, giant T waves,
     VT, marked ST elevation at high rate. The estimate then drifts toward
     the deflection and the whole signal acquires a DC offset which is
     INVISIBLE TO PCC (offset-invariant) and shows up only in RMSE. This is
     precisely why the metrics have to be read together.

4.4  RESAMPLING BIAS in align_length — the default was changed to
     "truncate" because "resample" flatters the digitiser. Stretching the
     reconstruction onto the reference's length is a time warp fitted from
     the very quantity being measured: if grid pitch was 2% off, so px_per_s
     was 2% off, resampling by length ratio silently UNDOES that error before
     any metric sees it.

     Measured, on a signal with a deliberate 2% time error:

       truncate   SNR  -2.08 dB     (correctly terrible)
       resample   SNR  +49.40 dB    (error erased)

     A 51 dB swing on the same pair of signals. "resample" is still
     available, for the case where the length difference is known to be a
     cropping artefact rather than a calibration one.

5.2  THE MOST IMPORTANT RESULT HERE is the gain column. The classical tracer
     SYSTEMATICALLY UNDER-READS AMPLITUDE — by 40% at 4 px/mm, and still by
     4.4% at 16 px/mm — while grid pitch is recovered essentially perfectly.

     The cause is column-centroid tracing. On a steep QRS upstroke a single
     pixel column contains a tall vertical run of ink, and its centroid sits
     at the run's midpoint, so the R peak is clipped by roughly half the
     within-column excursion. It is resolution-dependent, it is not a
     calibration fault, and it is the dominant amplitude error of the
     classical path. It is also the obvious thing a learned tracer would be
     expected to beat, which makes it a useful number for the thesis.

     Without gain_ratio this shows up only as elevated RMSE and would
     plausibly have been misread as a calibration bug for some time.

     8 px/mm (run_ptbxl.py):

9. B5 FIXED — the renderer no longer clips in silence
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

9.1  THE DEFECT. synth.render_printout defaulted to height_mm=40, which at
     10 mm/mV is +/-2.0 mV. cv2.polylines clips at the canvas edge without
     raising, so an over-tall trace flattened against the boundary rather
     than vanishing. Coverage still read ~100% and nothing warned.

     Why it mattered more than an ordinary rendering bug: the project's
     headline result is that the classical tracer under-reads amplitude
     (gain ratio 0.87 on real records). Canvas clipping pushes gain in the
     SAME direction, so the two were confounded.

9.2  HOW MUCH OF PTB-XL DOES NOT FIT. Surveyed over the whole test fold —
     2,198 records, 100 Hz, all 12 leads, 26,376 lead-traces. Half-extent
     required is max(|min|, |max|), because an ECG is not centred on zero.

       lead-traces needing more than +/-2.0 mV   1,971 / 26,376    7.5%
       records with at least one such lead         892 /  2,198   40.6%

       worst leads   V4 20.2%   V2 17.7%   V5 16.3%   V3 16.1%   V6 7.3%
       best leads    AVR 0.1%   I 0.5%   AVL 1.0%   II 1.4%

       per-lead half-extent   p50 0.93   p99 3.28   max 12.97 mV
       worst lead per record  p50 1.84   p99 5.25   max 12.97 mV

     Asymmetry is real and large: |min| exceeds |max| on 94.7% of AVR
     traces and 93.2% of V1. Sizing a canvas from signal.max() alone would
     under-size precisely the leads that overflow most.

9.3  THE CHANGE. synth.py only.

     - CLEARANCE_MM = 2.0 and DEFAULT_HEIGHT_MM = 70.0 added.
     - required_height_mm(signal, mm_per_mv, clearance_mm) added — the fit
       computation, from actual min and max.
     - render_printout gains on_overflow="raise"|"expand"|"clip", default
       "raise", and clearance_mm. Its height_mm default moves 40 -> 70.
     - RenderSpec gains v_min_mv, v_max_mv, required_height_mm and clipped,
       plus height_mm and headroom_mm properties. All four new fields have
       defaults, so layout.py's hand-built RenderSpec is unaffected.

     WHY 70 mm, i.e. the 99th percentile. Height costs blank paper and
     nothing else — it does not change px_per_mv, so amplitude resolution is
     untouched and only the pixel count grows. But the tail is brutal: p100
     would need 263 mm for a handful of records. 70 mm covers 99% of
     lead-traces, and because the default is now "raise" the residual 1%
     leaves the sample loudly and countably rather than quietly biased.
     Measured attrition on V2, the second-worst lead: 1 skip in 40 records.

9.4  HOW MUCH OF THE DEFICIT WAS THE RENDERER? This is the question Part 1
     had to answer. Method: score each trace twice, identically except for
     the renderer — (A) height_mm=40, on_overflow="clip", the behaviour the

9.7  A SECOND CONFOUND IN THE RESOLUTION SWEEP — REPORTED, NOT FIXED.
     The task asked whether trace thickness scales with resolution. IT DOES
     NOT. trace_thickness is a fixed 2 px at every px_per_mm, so the ink is
     a different physical width at every point in the sweep:

       4 px/mm -> 2 px = 0.500 mm        16 px/mm -> 2 px = 0.125 mm

     A 4x physical variation across the sweep. And ink width is the most
     influential parameter in the entire experiment. At a FIXED 8 px/mm,
     varying only thickness:

       thickness    1p     2p     3p     4p     5p     6p
       = mm      0.125  0.250  0.375  0.500  0.625  0.750
       gain        nan  0.864  0.725  0.726  0.540  0.541
       SNR dB      nan  13.81   7.32   7.32   3.19   3.16
       coverage   3.4%   100%   100%   100%   100%   100%

     A swing of 0.32 in gain from ink width alone — more than twice the
     whole amplitude deficit under study. (1 px collapses entirely: the
     anti-aliased line is too faint for Otsu, coverage 3.4%, another B2
     NaN.)

     Holding ink at a physically constant 0.3 mm instead, the sweep's
     trend REVERSES above 8 px/mm:

       px/mm            4.0    6.0    8.0   12.0   16.0
       gain @ 2 px     0.598  0.793  0.864  0.928  0.956
       gain @ 0.3 mm     nan  0.793  0.864  0.838  0.759

     So the clean monotone "more pixels per millimetre is better" in 9.6 is
     at least partly an artefact of the line getting physically thinner as
     resolution rises. A defensible resolution sweep must hold physical ink
     width constant.

     Related, and also unfixed: draw_grid writes 1-pixel rules at every
     resolution, so the grid has no physical width either (0.250 mm at
     4 px/mm, 0.062 mm at 16). Real ECG paper rules are ~0.1-0.2 mm.
     render_printout has no notion of physical line width at all —
     everything but the geometry is specified in pixels.

     NOT FIXED because it changes the meaning of every number in the
     resolution sweep, and that is a decision to take deliberately rather
     than as a side effect of a clipping fix.

     digitise(grid=...) so the pitch it reports is exactly the pitch used.

10.2 TRUE PHYSICAL SCALE, and how it is achieved. px_per_mm = dpi / 25.4
     (11.811 at the 300 dpi default). The page is composed at that scale and
     written through Pillow with an explicit resolution, so the PDF MediaBox
     is the true physical size. Verified by parsing the MediaBox back out:

       841.92 x 595.20 pt  =  297.011 x 209.973 mm
       A4 landscape        =  297.000 x 210.000 mm
       error               =  +0.011 / -0.027 mm   (0.004% / 0.013%)

     That residual is whole-pixel rounding of the page canvas and is three
     orders of magnitude below the few-percent errors that "Fit to page"
     introduces. The strip's own size is not rounded in the manifest: it is
     recovered back from the pixel dimensions actually produced
     (260.01 x 70.02 mm), and that recovered value is the ground truth
     scoring uses.

     DPI is used at print time, where a pixel really is 1/dpi inch, and then
     thrown away. Nothing downstream infers scale from image DPI — Reyna et
     al. are explicit that the correspondence does not hold for camera
     capture, which is the whole reason the grid has to be the ruler.

10.3 WHAT IS ON THE PAGE, and why in that arrangement.

       header text         record identity, calibration, dpi, git commit,
                           and the instruction to print at 100%
       THE STRIP           the gridded region -- the only thing to crop to
       crop marks          L-shaped, at the four strip corners, pointing
                           AWAY from the strip
       ruler band          100 mm horizontal, 50 mm vertical, 10 mm = 1 mV

     Everything except the trace sits outside the strip deliberately.
     digitise traces every column it is given and cannot reject non-trace
     ink, so a calibration pulse or a label inside the strip would be
     digitised as signal. The crop marks' inner vertex sits exactly on the
     strip corner without putting ink inside it — "the corner of the grid"
     is a judgement call at photograph resolution, a printed vertex is not.

     The first draft had the 50 mm bar centred on the ruler band, which put
     it INSIDE the strip. Caught on inspection and fixed; the band now hangs
     downward from below the crop marks.

     Three references rather than one, because they fail differently: the
     100 mm bar catches uniform scaling ("Fit to page", the likeliest
     fault), the 50 mm vertical catches ANISOTROPIC scaling that a
     horizontal check alone would miss, and the 10 mm = 1 mV bar states the
     same ruler in the unit the result is reported in.

10.4 RECTIFICATION IS MANUAL AND DELIBERATELY SO. Four corners are supplied
     (--corners, --pick, or corners_px in the manifest), not detected.
     Automatic page detection and deskew are a later pipeline stage; building
     them here would confound the measurement with the quality of a detector
     nobody has written. This is the manual stand-in, not the real thing.

     The warp output size comes ONLY from the photograph's own corner
     geometry — the mean length of each pair of opposite edges. It never
     uses the strip's known aspect ratio. Forcing the true aspect would hand
     the estimator half the answer it is being tested on and would erase the
     anisotropy that perspective introduces, which is one of the things
     worth measuring.

     True px/mm for scoring = warp output size / the strip's known physical
     size. Ground truth is used to SCORE the estimate, never to produce it.

10.5 TOOLING SELF-TEST ON SIMULATED PHOTOGRAPHS. No print has been made and
     no photograph taken. To prove the two scripts run end to end before a
     human spends time printing, the rendered page was put through a
     synthetic camera: perspective warp, 2.5 degrees of rotation, lens blur,
     illumination gradient, sensor noise, JPEG 88, at several frame sizes.

     THESE ARE NOT PHOTOGRAPHS. No ink bleed, no paper texture, no fibre, no
     thermal fade, no specular highlight, no rolling shutter, no chromatic
     aberration, and the corner coordinates are exact rather than clicked.
     The simulation is strictly easier than reality and every number from it
     is optimistic. It proves the pipeline runs; it measures nothing about
     the field.

     A 12 MP simulated capture, square-ish on, scored:

       rectified        2893 x 786 px from a 4032 x 3024 frame
       true px/mm       11.126 horizontal, 11.226 vertical
       estimated        11.127 (+0.01%),  11.264 (+0.34%)

10.6 THE SELF-TEST FOUND SOMETHING REAL: A SILENT 5x HARMONIC LOCK.

     At an 8 MP frame, the COMBINATION of lens blur, JPEG compression and an

10.7 A NEW FINDING, REPORTED NOT FIXED — Metrics.diagnose() misattributes.
     In one locked run (x on 2x, y on 5x) PCC came out at -0.069, i.e.
     essentially uncorrelated, and diagnose() reported:

       "inverted: shape matches but sign is flipped -- check the
        pixels-to-mV sign convention"

     The sign convention was fine. diagnose() branches on `pcc < 0.0`, so
     any trivially negative correlation is reported as a sign inversion and
     sends the reader to debug the one thing that is not wrong. Claiming
     inversion should require a strongly negative PCC (say < -0.7); between
     roughly -0.7 and +0.7 the honest answer is that the tracer lost the
     waveform. Call this B9. Not fixed: evaluate.py is outside this task.

10.8 OBSERVATION: mild blur HELPS the centroid tracer. At 12 MP the clean
     warp scored PCC 0.875 / gain 0.846, and adding lens blur and JPEG
     improved it to 0.986 / 0.946. A hard-edged resampled line gives Otsu a
     ragged mask; a slightly blurred one binarises to a smoother, more
     symmetric run whose centroid is steadier. Worth remembering before
     anyone "improves" capture by sharpening.

10.9 WHAT A HUMAN MUST DO BEFORE ANY REAL NUMBER EXISTS. In order, and none
     of it is optional:

       1. Run make_print_target.py. Keep the .pdf, _truth.npy and
          _manifest.json together — the .npy is what the photograph is
          scored against and without it the photograph is unmeasurable.
       2. Print at 100% scale, in COLOUR, on plain matte A4, landscape.
          Turn OFF "Fit to page" / "Shrink oversized pages" — they are on by
          default in most print dialogs. Greyscale breaks binarise_trace
          outright: the grid becomes neutral and is returned AS the trace.
       3. Measure the printed 100 mm and 50 mm bars with a real ruler.
          Tolerance +/-0.5 mm. If either is wrong, reprint — do not
          photograph it and do not try to correct afterwards. A scaled print
          is a ruler of unknown length and nothing in the image reveals the
          factor.
       4. Photograph per docs/photo_protocol.md: flat matte surface, EVEN
          DIFFUSE LIGHT (see 10.6 — uneven light is what broke the grid
          estimate in simulation), highest resolution, HDR off, flash off,
          focus on the grid. Nine shots: three identical square-on repeats
          first, then two rotations, two tilts, one deliberately lit from
          one side, one dim handheld.
       5. Transfer at full resolution. Not through WhatsApp, Messenger,
          Slack or iMessage — all recompress and several downscale.
       6. Run score_photo.py --pick --save-corners --diagnostics, and LOOK
          at the overlay image before believing any number.

     Until step 4 happens there is no real-image measurement and this
     changelog should not be read as containing one.

9.1 DESKEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Method: projection profile sharpness maximisation. The grid is a periodic
lattice of straight lines; axis-aligned, its projection onto an image axis
is a comb of sharp peaks, and rotation smears them together. Sharpness of
the projection is therefore a function of angle with a maximum at the true
skew.

The search never warps the image. Binning pixels by y - x*tan(a) collects
any line of slope a into one bin, and because rounding that index makes the
shift constant over runs of adjacent columns, each projection is one
grouped column sum plus a few hundred shifted vector adds. No interpolation
happens during the search, which matters because interpolation is a
low-pass filter acting on exactly the detail being measured. At a = 0 the
operation is identical to the projection estimate_grid_pitch already takes.

Coarse pass: +/-10 deg at 0.5 deg on a copy reduced to 900 px longest side.
Fine pass: +/-0.75 deg at 0.05 deg at full resolution, then a parabolic fit
to the objective for sub-step precision.

RESULTS (results/deskew/):

  Angle recovery, 3 fixtures x 17 true angles over +/-8 deg:
    max abs error 0.0043 deg, median 0.00000, mean 0.00034.
    Worst cases are all on the 150 dpi fixture, the lowest-resolution one.

  End-to-end, 12 PTB-XL test-fold records, lead II, 8 px/mm strips:
    unrotated            SNR 8.58  RMSE 0.0494  PCC 0.973  gain 0.869
    rotated 1 deg        12/12 FAIL (grid pitch NaN)
    deskewed 1 deg       SNR 8.60  RMSE 0.0503  PCC 0.963  gain 0.857
    round-trip control   SNR 8.49  RMSE 0.0503  PCC 0.963  gain 0.857
    rotated 3 deg        12/12 FAIL
    deskewed 3 deg       SNR 8.35  RMSE 0.0493  PCC 0.970  gain 0.864
    round-trip control   SNR 8.36  RMSE 0.0493  PCC 0.970  gain 0.864

    Deskew matches the round-trip control to three decimals. The control
    rotates and unrotates by the *known* angle, so it carries the
    interpolation cost and none of the estimation error. The two agreeing
    means the angle estimate contributes nothing measurable, and the whole
    residual gap to the unrotated condition -- about 1 point of PCC and 1.2
    of gain -- is the price of one interpolation.

  Failure boundaries, all at 2 deg unless stated:
    angle          exact to +/-44 deg; aliases by 90 deg at 46 deg, which
                   is the square lattice symmetry itself, not a defect
    blur           exact to sigma = 8 px (margin falls 569 -> 33)
    JPEG           exact to quality 5 (margin falls 464 -> 52)
    grid fade      exact down to 10% saturation; FAILS at 0% (pure grey)
                   with an explicit error, and a luminance response_fn
                   recovers the same 2.000 deg with margin 307 -- the
                   method is colour-agnostic, only the default front end
                   is red-specific
    grid coverage  exact to 15% of frame (error 0.0018 deg), but the
                   margin collapses 337 -> 12.4, so margin is the signal
                   to watch

  Cost. estimate_skew against estimate_grid_pitch, median of 3:
    2.10 MP    195 / 347 ms   vs  29 / 32 ms     x6.8 - x11.0
    3.74 MP    372 / 540 ms   vs  50 / 55 ms     x7.4 -  x9.9
    8.41 MP   1691 / 1941 ms  vs 130 / 139 ms   x13.0 - x14.0
    (first figure at 0 deg, second at 3 deg; cost grows with |angle|
    because the shear spans more bins.)

    This is the headline problem. Deskew is 7-14x the cost of what was
    already the most expensive stage, and it is superlinear in pixels.
    On a phone this does not fit as written. The coarse pass is cheap; the
    fine pass at full resolution is where the time goes, and reducing its
    resolution is the obvious lever. Not attempted yet.

  Sharpness measure. variance and ssd both reach ~0.001 deg accuracy;
  ssd has 4.6x the median peak prominence (660 vs 143) at equal cost, so
  ssd is the default. kurtosis fails outright (median error 3.9 deg).
  entropy is accurate but its margin is not interpretable, being a ratio
  of two negative numbers.

  Interpolation, simulation held fixed at Lanczos:
    lanczos   pitch shift 0.064%  contrast 0.919  PCC 0.9697  gain 0.8779
    nearest               0.070%           0.949      0.9628      0.8727
    bicubic               0.075%           0.949      0.9592      0.8693
    bilinear              0.090%           0.926      0.9537      0.8647

    Lanczos is the default on the waveform metrics. The robust finding is
    negative: bilinear, the OpenCV default, is worst on every axis.

9.2 A MISTAKE WORTH RECORDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The first interpolation comparison varied the warp used to *simulate* the
test rotation together with the warp used to *correct* it. It concluded
nearest-neighbour won on every axis by a wide margin, and that conclusion
was written into the module as its default before the flaw was noticed. An
image simulated with nearest arrives at the correction step less blurred,
so nearest was being credited for its own simulation. With the simulation
pinned to Lanczos the ordering changes and Lanczos wins.

run_deskew.py now has separate --interp and --sim-interp flags. CLAUDE.md
gained a convention line about it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.3 B1 GUARDS, AND WHAT THEY CANNOT DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

GridEstimate.confidence and .anisotropy were documented as the way to
distrust a pitch estimate and were consulted by nothing. digitise.py was
modified to close that, which is the whole of what was changed there:

  - GridEstimate gains spacing_cv (default NaN, so existing construction
    sites keep working); _pitch_1d returns a fourth value to supply it.
  - New CalibrationCheck dataclass and check_calibration function.
  - digitise() gains expected_duration_s and strict (default True) and
    calls the check; DigitiseResult gains .calibration.

Guards: an axis that failed to resolve, anisotropy above 0.15, and implied
paper speed outside 15-60 mm/s. spacing_cv is advisory only.

The measurements say plainly what does and does not work.

confidence does not discriminate. It sat at 0.68 on an estimate that was
399% wrong and 0.63 on one that was correct. The reason is structural:
peaks are selected as a fraction of the maximum, so the survivors are tall
by construction and their mean height is close to a constant of the
algorithm.

spacing_cv does not discriminate either, in the way that matters:
    render at 4.0 px/mm    pitch correct to -0.0%    spacing_cv 0.412
    fixture at 200 dpi     pitch wrong by +399%      spacing_cv 0.184
It is high on a good estimate at low resolution (quantisation) and low on
a bad one (an unresolved minor grid is a genuinely regular comb of 5 mm
rules). A major/minor autocorrelation height ratio was also tried and
fails the same way: 1.21 and 1.31 on correct estimates, 1.48 on one that
was 4x wrong. Hence advisory, not a rejection criterion.

The paper-speed check is the one that works, and it works because it
brings in a fact from outside the image:
    fixture 200 dpi clean   implied 5.6 mm/s   REJECTED  (true err +399%)
    fixture blurred/JPEG    implied 5.6 mm/s   REJECTED
    render at 8.0 px/mm     implied 26.0 mm/s  accepted  (true err -0.0%)
    render at 4.0 px/mm     implied 26.0 mm/s  accepted  (true err -0.0%)
    render at 2.5 px/mm     implied  6.5 mm/s  REJECTED  (true err +300%)
No false positives and no false negatives across the eleven conditions
tested. It is skipped, with a warning saying so, when the caller supplies
no expected_duration_s -- which is most callers today.

This confirms what the estimate_grid_pitch docstring already predicted:
nothing inside the autocorrelation can distinguish a 1 mm grid from a 5 mm
grid misread as 1 mm, because they are the same picture at different
distances.

Three things this surfaced that were not previously recorded.

FIRST, and new: binarise_trace finishes with a 3x3 morphological opening,
and erosion by a 3x3 kernel deletes any ink run that is not 3 px thick in
both directions. On the 200 dpi rhythm strip the trace is ~3 px and column
coverage goes from 100% thresholded to 24.9% after the opening; digitise
then returns a signal largely NaN and every metric comes back NaN. At
300 dpi the trace is ~4 px and coverage survives at 99.8%. This is NOT the
red-channel dependency of CLAUDE.md limitation 2 -- thresholding is fine
either way -- it is the despeckling step eating a thin line, and it sets a
hard lower bound on usable capture resolution that nothing had measured.

SECOND, confirming CLAUDE.md limitation 6 in the field: peak_fraction=0.20
returns 41.30 px/mm on the 300 dpi crop against a true 11.81, and the
guards reject it on both anisotropy (0.858) and implied paper speed
(12.5 mm/s). At peak_fraction=0.03 it returns 11.8262, +0.13%. The B1
guards did their job on a real image, in both directions.

THIRD, artefacts the tracer cannot distinguish from signal:
  - the lead-name label is dark neutral ink inside the ROI and is traced
    as a waveform. Suppressed here by painting the ground-truth
    text_bounding_box white, which is only available when the image was
    generated with --lead_name_bbox. poc_* was not, so the label survives
    as a spike at t=0 in the wrinkled figure.
  - the calibration pulse trailing edge sits exactly where plotted_pixels
    begins; the crop now starts 1 mm later.
  - on the wrinkled image a dark stain near t=4.3 s produces a flat-topped
    plateau roughly 0.3 mV high. The tracer follows the stain. Nothing in
    the pipeline detects this, and it is the clearest single argument for
    a learned tracer that has seen dirty paper.

Suppressing the label and the pulse edge moved the clean case from
SNR 5.57 / PCC 0.9272 to SNR 7.47 / PCC 0.9775.

Figures land in out/demo/ and are regenerable, so they are not committed.

NARROWING THE ANGULAR RANGE -- helped, and is applied. The coarse pass

|coarse - truth| observed is exactly 0.2500, never more than half a step.

rather than one and a half: a third off the fine pass for a worst-case
error of 0.00433 against 0.00428. It is NOT cut to 0.25, which the
measurement would allow, because a coarse pass that ever did miss by a step
would put the true peak outside the fine window and fail silently and
large, and the remaining saving is 20 ms of a stage that now costs 85.

COARSENING THE FINE STEP -- did not help. The parabola fit is sub-step, so
sampling it more thinly looks free and is not. Median error stays at 1e-5
throughout; it is the worst case that goes, and step 0.375 already breaches
the requirement at 0.108 degrees. Compare like for like in the table above:
step 0.25 costs 76.0 ms for a worst case of 0.0754, where resolution 900
costs 28.4 ms for 0.0408. Less than half the time and half the error.

EARLY TERMINATION -- did not help. Its minimum-cost form is exactly the
three points the parabola is fitted to, and it is in the table: 34.4 ms for
0.0755 degrees, against 28.4 ms for 0.0408 from resolution alone. Cheaper
and more accurate on the other axis, so there is nothing left for early
termination to buy. Widening the bracket to make the three points cover
more angle makes it worse, not better: at half_range 0.375 the worst case
is 0.125 degrees, outside the requirement.

RESTRICTING THE PROJECTION TO A SUBREGION -- did not help, and is the one
that looked most promising. Compared against downsampling the whole frame
to the same pixel count, so the comparison is at equal cost, it wins at the
smallest budget (0.0474 against 0.0748) and loses at the two larger ones
(0.0312 vs 0.0259, 0.0390 vs 0.0277). Its MEDIAN error is roughly twice as
high at every budget, which says the inconsistency is real and not one
unlucky fixture. Cropping trades lever arm for resolution and the objective
wants both; there is no free side to that trade, and adding a parameter to
buy an inconsistent 20% was not worth it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11.4 THE GRID PITCH THRESHOLD: THE CHANNEL HYPOTHESIS IS WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The premise going in was that GridEstimator.kt reads the GREEN channel and
gets the right pitch on the ecg-image-kit fixtures where the Python's
redness response gets a fivefold error, and that green must therefore
preserve a better minor-to-major contrast ratio. Both halves of that are
false, and the measurement is in results/grid/.

FIRST. The Kotlin does not get them right. Running its rule unmodified --
grid_reference.py is its line-for-line twin, and the vectorised projection
used here matches its own pixel loop to 0.0 exactly -- it returns NaN on all
three fixtures, finding 0 or 1 peaks where it requires 3. It gets the
synthetic renders right, exactly as the Python does. The two
implementations do not disagree; they fail on the same images, and the only
difference is that the Kotlin fails loudly and the Python returns 39.26
px/mm against a true 7.874 with every internal diagnostic healthy.

SECOND. The two thresholds were never comparable, and neither is a fraction
of anything about the grid. The Python thresholds at
peak_fraction * ac[:hi].max(), and because the autocorrelation is
normalised by its zero lag that maximum is always exactly 1.0 -- so 0.20 was
an absolute 0.20. The Kotlin thresholds at 0.25 * max(acf[3:60]), and that
window maximum measures 0.71-0.95 on these images, so its cut lands at
0.178-0.238. The same place. Two knobs that look different, spelled
differently, doing the same thing.

THIRD. Green is the WORST of the four channels tested, not the best.
results/grid/contrast.csv, the largest threshold at which all four minor
peaks below the first major one survive:

    source              blue    redness   green    luma
    gt_norm_150dpi     0.1228   0.1173   0.1030   0.0998
    gt_mi_200dpi       0.1229   0.1111   0.1018   0.1007
    gt_sttc_300dpi     0.1805   0.1681   0.1546   0.1524
    synth 6-16 px/mm   0.78-0.83   same     same    same

The reason is in the ink. ecg-image-kit draws minor rules at BGR
(219,223,254) and major rules at (0,0,255), so 255-B gives the minors
36/255 of the majors' response and 255-G gives 32/255. Blue's edge is 12%
and it is an artefact of one generator's palette. The moving-average
detrend the Kotlin applies moves the number by about 7% and changes nothing
qualitative.

SO, TO THE QUESTION AS POSED -- does the channel choice remove the need for
a low threshold, or merely shift where it sits? Neither, quite. It shifts
it, by about 20% between the best and worst channel, and it shifts green in
the wrong direction. What decides the outcome is something larger than the
channel: the kit's minor rules are roughly seven times fainter than its
majors and this repository's synthetic renders draw them at near-equal
weight, so the required threshold differs by a factor of EIGHT between
image sources where it differs by 20% between channels. That is CLAUDE.md
limitation 6's "synthetic images flatter the pipeline", quantified.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11.5 RECOMMENDED CONFIGURATION, AND THE BEFORE-AND-AFTER IT REQUIRED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommendation: keep the REDNESS response, and lower peak_fraction from
0.20 to 0.05. Same in both ports.

Redness rather than blue, despite blue measuring marginally better, because
blue's advantage is 12% and specific to one generator's ink, while redness
has an independent structural reason -- it is near-silent on the neutral
trace, so the profile it produces is grid and not grid-plus-waveform -- and
because at the resolution floor redness recovers a 3.0 px/mm render where
blue returns NaN.

0.05 rather than 0.03 or 0.10, from results/grid/robust.csv: it is the
highest threshold at which every channel tested recovers every clean source
correctly, it sits a factor of two below the lowest value any fixture
requires (0.1007), and 0.01 returns the same pitch to four decimal places,
so there is nothing below it worth having. It also fails better at the
resolution floor: a 2.5 px/mm render returns a confident 197-300% error at
0.10-0.20 and NaN at 0.05.

WHAT NO THRESHOLD FIXES, and it should be said before the gains. On the kit
fixtures the 1 mm rules do not survive blur past sigma 1 or JPEG below
quality 20 at all: at sigma 3 no channel and no threshold recovers the
pitch, because the information is no longer in the image. Lowering the
threshold buys the clean case and buys nothing beyond it.

This is a change to peak selection in the control condition, so, as
required, before and after on every existing result.

GAINED -- results/deskew/guards.csv, the clean 200 dpi fixture:

                       peak_fraction 0.20   peak_fraction 0.05
    px/mm recovered          39.2599              7.9021
    error vs true             +398.6%              +0.4%
    implied paper speed       5.6 mm/s            27.8 mm/s
    check_calibration         REJECTED            ACCEPTED

and results/deskew/interp.csv, unrotated fixture pitch, all three:
39.301 -> 7.914 (+0.5%), 29.378 -> 5.950 (+0.7%), 50.186 -> 11.823 (+0.1%).
The fixtures calibrate correctly for the first time. Confirmed on a real
ecg-image-kit printout rather than only the bundled fixtures:
demo_kit_pipeline.py on gt_300dpi 00009_hr now recovers 11.8374 px/mm
(+0.22%) at the default where it previously needed peak_fraction=0.03
passed by hand, SNR 7.48 dB / PCC 0.9775 / gain 0.944.

UNCHANGED -- results/deskew/endtoend.csv, 12 records, every condition that
produced a result at 0.20: max per-record delta PCC exactly 0.0, delta gain
1e-4, delta SNR 0.001 dB, px_per_mm_x identical. angle.csv and
measures.csv are bit-identical (deskew does not call estimate_grid_pitch).
Synthetic renders from 3.0 to 16.0 px/mm return the same pitch to four
decimal places at every threshold from 0.01 to 0.20.

LOST, and this is a real cost. At 0.20 a one-degree rotation made grid
estimation fail outright and digitise() refused -- CLAUDE.md limitation 7's
"12/12 records". At 0.05 it succeeds, returns a waveform at PCC 0.49-0.77,
and check_calibration accepts 10 of those 12 because the pitch really is
right; the other 2 are caught on anisotropy, their y-axis having locked
onto a harmonic. Rotation was never a tolerable input and deskew runs first
by design, but the accidental safety net that used to sit at grid
estimation is gone, and a rotated frame now fails on PCC downstream instead
of on calibration here.

NOT DONE, deliberately. The peak SELECTION RULE is unchanged. The case for
changing it is strong and is recorded here rather than acted on: no fixed
height threshold can be right for both image families when the required
value differs eightfold between them, and a rule that tested a candidate
fundamental against its own harmonics -- rather than against an absolute
height -- would not have this failure mode at all. That is a different
algorithm and needs its own before-and-after.

FOR THE KOTLIN PORT, which was not modified, three changes are required to
match:
  1. read the redness response, r - 0.5*(g + b) clipped at zero, not
     255 - green;
  2. threshold at an absolute 0.05 of the zero-lag-normalised
     autocorrelation, dropping the 0.25 * windowMax rule entirely, since
     the Python's threshold is absolute and matching it means being
     absolute;
  3. raise MAX_PX_PER_MM, currently 40, and with it the lag search window,
     currently min(60, n-2). At 300 dpi the 5 mm rule sits at 59.06 px and
     is at the very edge of that window; the Python searches to 480.
The moving-average detrend can stay or go -- it moves the numbers by about
7% and nothing qualitative depends on it -- but whichever is chosen must be
chosen in both, and grid_reference.py must be updated with it so that the
twin stays a twin.

11.6 TWO LATENT BUGS FOUND WHILE MEASURING, BOTH FIXED, NEITHER CHANGING
     ANY EXISTING NUMBER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FIRST, digitise._pitch_1d raised IndexError as soon as the threshold went
low enough to admit an autocorrelation peak below the minimum lag:

    peaks, props = sps.find_peaks(...)
    peaks = peaks[peaks >= min_pitch_px]          # peaks rebound here
    ...
    heights = props["peak_heights"][peaks >= min_pitch_px]   # wrong length

After the rebinding the mask is all-true and of the FILTERED length, and
indexing the unfiltered props with it raises. It could never fire at the
0.20 default -- a peak below lag 3 would need a normalised autocorrelation
above 0.20 at lag 1 or 2 -- so it sat latent until the threshold sweep. The
mask is now held. It affects the `confidence` field only; `pitch` is
computed from `peaks` and cannot change. Verified: guards.csv, failure.csv,
interp.csv and endtoend.csv are byte-identical across the fix.

SECOND, run_deskew.py recorded calibration outcomes as

    bool(res.calibration.ok) if res.calibration else None

and CalibrationCheck defines __bool__ as its own `ok`. So `if
res.calibration` is false for exactly the rejected estimates the column
exists to record, and every rejection was written to the CSV as blank
rather than as False. Now `is not None`. The previously-committed
endtoend.csv under-reported calibration failures for this reason.

Also, not a bug but a provenance gap: the JSON sidecars recorded CLI
arguments and library versions but not the module constants a result
actually depends on, so a sweep that varies FINE_MAX_DIM while
FINE_HALF_RANGE_DEG sits at whatever the module held that day could not be
reconstructed from its own sidecar. provenance() now emits a
module_defaults block with the seven deskew and four digitise constants.

12.1 OPENCV, AND WHAT IT COSTS TO SHIP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Added org.opencv:opencv:4.14.0, the official Maven artifact, loaded once
from Application.onCreate via OpenCVLoader.initLocal() and reported on
screen. The load verdict is held in OpenCvRuntime rather than attempted at
first use, because OpenCV's Java API is a JNI shim: if the native library
is absent nothing fails at the point of the mistake, it fails later as an
UnsatisfiedLinkError from inside whichever Mat operation happened to run
first. OpenCvRuntime catches Throwable, not Exception, because
UnsatisfiedLinkError is an Error and would otherwise pass straight through.

    + OpenCV, all four ABIs           164,712,971 B    157.08 MiB  +144.57

The reason is visible in the artifact: libopencv_java4.so is 24.7 MB for

and native libraries are stored uncompressed in the APK. The target device

12.2 A ROTATION CONTROL, WHICH IS THE GROUND TRUTH MECHANISM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A slider on DigitiseScreen injects a rotation of plus-or-minus 10 degrees in
0.1 degree detents, applied with Imgproc.warpAffine and displayed.

This is not a viewer control. It is how ground truth gets onto the device.
A bundled fixture has a known grid pitch, so the grid estimator can be
scored against it on the phone; but a fixture's true SKEW is only ever
zero, so there is nothing for a skew estimator to be right or wrong about
until a known angle is put in deliberately. The slider is that, and stage
12.4 reads the angle back out.

The range is plus-or-minus 10 degrees because that is the Python's
COARSE_HALF_RANGE_DEG: the slider covers the estimator's stated operating
range and stops at its edge. The detent is 0.1 degrees because that is the
residual the reconstruction can absorb -- 1 degree of uncorrected rotation
takes PCC from 0.981 to 0.734 -- so a finer control would be below what the
rest of the pipeline can distinguish.

ImageWarp.kt mirrors deskew.rotation_matrix and deskew.rotate_image, and
keeps the Python's three departures from OpenCV's defaults, none of which
are cosmetic: expand the canvas rather than crop the corners (cropping
removes trace from a strip that runs to both ends, and the digitiser then
scores a shorter waveform without saying so); fill the border white rather
than black (the grid response keys on red saturation, for which white is
silent and a black margin would contribute a hard straight edge to exactly
the statistic the angle search maximises); and interpolate with Lanczos
rather than bilinear (measured in results/deskew/interp.csv -- bilinear,
the OpenCV default, is the worst of four on every axis).

Two things that are about the device rather than the algorithm:

collectLatest, not collect. A slider drag emits a value per frame and a
Lanczos warp of an 8 MP image takes far longer than a frame, so plain
collection would queue dozens of warps and the image would keep moving for
seconds after the finger stopped. Cancelling the previous collection keeps
at most one warp in flight.

Full-resolution bitmaps are never drawn. The 300 dpi fixture is 33.7 MB as
ARGB_8888, and an original plus a rotated plus (in 12.4) a corrected copy
is over 100 MB against a per-app heap of roughly 200 MB on a 3 GB device.
Processing happens on Mats, which are native allocations the Java heap does
not see; only a 1080 px preview reaches the screen. Mats are released in
finally blocks, because the garbage collector does not feel native pressure
and will not free them in time under repeated use.

At zero degrees no warp is applied at all -- the original is returned
unchanged rather than warped by zero, since every interpolation is a
low-pass filter on the grid detail the later stages measure.

NOT VERIFIED: that any of this behaves on hardware. Compile only.

12.3 TWO DEVICE-CONFIRMED BUGS, ONE OF THEM PREDICTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

First run on the A30s. The image rotates and does so in acceptable time, so
12.2 works. Two faults, and the second is a result rather than a defect.

A. "Rotate failed: Child of the scoped flow was cancelled", permanently.

Self-inflicted, in the code written yesterday. The warp was wrapped in
runCatching, which catches Throwable -- and that includes
CancellationException, which is not a failure but the NORMAL outcome of
collectLatest. Every slider value cancels the warp in flight, so during a
drag nearly every iteration ends in cancellation; each one wrote a failure
into the status line, and the single iteration that did succeed had no way
to clear it. Now caught separately and rethrown, which also matters beyond
the message: swallowing a CancellationException leaves the coroutine
machinery believing a cancelled child is still live.

B. Grid pitch "failed" on all three fixtures.

Not new, and not a surprise. CLAUDE.md limitation 6 predicted exactly this
from the Python side on 2026-09-17: GridEstimator.kt "returns NaN on all
three, because its 0.25 * max(acf[3:60]) cut lands at 0.178-0.238 -- the
same place as the Python's 0.20". This is that prediction confirmed on
hardware, which is worth more than the fix: the failure was derived from a
desktop measurement of the autocorrelation and then observed on the device
without any further tuning.

Fixed with the three changes limitation 6 named, and ONLY those three:

  1. redness (r - 0.5*(g+b), clipped) instead of 255 - green
  2. an absolute 0.05 threshold instead of 0.25 * windowMax
  3. MAX_PX_PER_MM 40 -> 80, widening the lag search window from
     MAX * 1.5 to MAX * 6, which is the Python's min(max_pitch_px * 6, ...)

Each was simulated against the three fixtures before being written, by
reimplementing the Kotlin's exact control flow in Python -- its detrend, its
peak test, its three-peak minimum, its median of successive gaps -- and
varying only the authorised parameters:

    configuration                        norm       mi      sttc
    shipped (green, 0.25*windowMax)       NaN      NaN       NaN
    (a) redness only                      NaN  +199.15%      NaN
    (b) + absolute 0.05                +0.20%   -0.08%    -0.15%
    (c) + wide lag window              +0.61%   +0.32%    +0.08%

Two things fall out of that table. The channel change ALONE fixes nothing --
(a) is still broken on all three -- which confirms for the third time, and
now in the port itself, that the channel was never the fault. And (c) sits
within 0.06 percentage points of what the Python returns on the same images
(+0.55%, +0.36%, +0.13%), which is the check that this is a port rather
than a reimplementation that happens to agree.

Synthetic renders at 6, 8, 12 and 16 px/mm are unaffected by every
configuration, so nothing regressed on the family the Python was developed
against.

NOT changed, deliberately: the moving-average detrend, which is not on the
authorised list. Recorded because it is now measured -- with the other three
changes in place, removing it moves the recovered pitch by under 0.15
percentage points on every fixture. It is the one remaining structural
difference between the two implementations and it does not matter.

WHAT THE 205 MS SAYS ABOUT THE DEVICE PROJECTION IN 12.3 -- WITH CARE.

Section 11.3 projected desktop-to-device at 8-12x, from clock and IPC
ratios. The only directly comparable pair now measured says less:

    estimate_grid_pitch, desktop, 8.41 MP, both axes    117.7 ms
    GridEstimator,       device,  8.41 MP, one axis     205   ms
                                          naive ratio     1.74x
                                          per-axis        3.48x

That is a factor of three better than assumed, and if it holds, deskew's
74-89 ms desktop becomes roughly 260-310 ms on the phone rather than
0.6-1.1 s.

Four reasons not to bank it yet. The two sides are not the same program:
the Python does both axes with OpenCV's SIMD channel split, NumPy's FFT and
SciPy's peak finder, while the Kotlin does one axis with getPixels, a
hand-written radix-2 FFT and a hand-written peak scan -- so the ratio mixes
hardware with implementation. It is a single reading, taken cold, with no
warm-up and no repetition. It is a debug build. And deskew has a different
shape again: an OpenCV resize plus a Kotlin sweep over an image reduced to
900 px, which is 28x fewer pixels than this measurement covers.

So the honest statement is that the 8-12x band was probably pessimistic and
the real figure is likelier 2-4x for this kind of work, and that stage 12.6
measures the deskew stage directly rather than inferring it. Recorded here
because a projection that was wrong by three-fold is worth correcting in
the record even before the better number exists.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12.5 SKEW ESTIMATOR PORTED TO KOTLIN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SkewEstimator.kt is ecgproto/deskew.py's estimate_skew, ported rather than
redesigned. Every constant is read from the Python: coarse half-range 10
degrees at 0.5 degree steps, fine half-range 0.5 at 0.05, both passes at
900 px, ssd objective, redness response taken once on the already-reduced
image.

DIVISION OF LABOUR, which follows from the algorithm rather than from taste.
OpenCV does the resize and the warp: both are full-resolution and per-pixel,
which is where a SIMD native routine wins and a Kotlin loop loses badly.
Kotlin does the response and the angle search, both of which run on an image
already reduced to 900 px. The search has no OpenCV equivalent -- it bins
pixels by y - x*tan(a), which rounds to a shift that is constant over runs
of adjacent columns, so each projection is a grouped column sum plus a few
hundred shifted adds, one pass over the image per angle with no
interpolation anywhere. Expressing that in OpenCV primitives would mean
warping once per angle, which is both slower and wrong: every warp
interpolates, and interpolation is a low-pass filter on exactly the fine
grid detail the objective measures.

REUSE. GridResponse now holds the redness arithmetic for both stages, which
reach the pixels by different routes -- packed ARGB ints from a Bitmap in
GridEstimator, RGBA bytes from a Mat in SkewEstimator -- and so offered two
chances to get the channel order wrong in a way that yields a response that
is wrong without looking wrong. GridEstimator was changed only to call it;
the arithmetic is identical and this is not one of the three algorithm
changes of 12.3. The rest of GridEstimator's machinery does NOT apply: its
projection is unsheared and its autocorrelation finds a period, where the
skew search needs the sharpness of a sheared projection. Different
quantities, no shared code beyond the response.

VERIFICATION, in two levels, because compiling says nothing about numerics.
The Kotlin was transcribed literally back into Python -- its fused
projection-and-sharpness loop, its Math.rint, its accumulator indexing, its
arange, its parabolic peak -- and run against deskew.py on the same inputs.

  Level 1, one sheared projection at a time, over eleven angles from -10 to
  +10 on a rotated 200 dpi fixture, both axes: relative difference exactly
  0.00e+00 at every angle. The fused loop is bit-identical to
  _sheared_projection followed by _sharpness.

  Level 2, the whole estimate over three fixtures and fourteen injected
  angles, exercising arange lengths, core lengths, the coarse argmax, the
  coarse-to-fine handoff and the parabolic peak: worst |python - kotlin| is
  7.1e-15 degrees, which is floating-point noise. Sweep lengths come out 41
  coarse and 21 fine, matching. Worst error against the injected truth is
  0.04080 degrees -- exactly the Python's published worst at 900 px, 0.0408.

Between the two levels every line of estimate() is covered.

THE LIMIT OF THAT CLAIM, stated because it is easy to overread: this
verifies the algorithm as transcribed back into Python, not the compiled
Kotlin running on the device. A mistake made identically in both directions
would survive it, and so would anything specific to the JVM -- Math.rint
semantics, integer overflow, the Mat-to-byte-array path. Stage 12.6 puts a
known angle in on the phone and reads the recovered one out, which is the
check that cannot be faked.

ONE DELIBERATE DIVERGENCE, and one assertion. The transpose is materialised
once and reused across all 65 angle evaluations; the Python re-slices
response.T per call, which is free there because NumPy transposes are views
but would mean a strided access on every evaluation here. And because both
passes run at 900 px in the shipped configuration, the Python's per-pass
_reduce of the response is the identity -- rather than implement a resampler
that cannot be exercised, estimate() requires the constants to say so and
fails loudly if either is ever set below the working size.

NOT VERIFIED: anything on hardware. Compile and desktop simulation only.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12.6 THE VERIFICATION LOOP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DeskewPipeline.run wires the slider to the estimator:

    inject a known rotation -> estimate the skew -> correct it -> display

and DigitiseScreen shows injected, recovered, absolute error, the peak
margin, the axis disagreement, the coarse angle, the working size, a
per-stage timing breakdown, and the original and corrected images side by
side.

WHY THIS IS A TEST AND NOT A DEMO. On the bench a rotation is injected and
recovered by the same script, so agreement proves only self-consistency.
Here the injected angle comes from a slider a human moved, the estimate runs
on a different processor in a different language, and there is a published
number to be scored against: the Python's worst case over three fixtures and
seventeen angles at a 900 px working resolution is 0.041 degrees. That
figure is printed in the panel next to the measurement, as a "vs Python
worst" row, because a number with nothing to compare against is not a test.
A device result materially worse than 0.041 indicates a porting error rather
than a property of the algorithm.

Two things about the arrangement that are deliberate.

The corrected image is shown NEXT TO the original, never alone. A corrected
image on its own always looks plausible -- the grid is straight because the
warp made it straight, whatever angle it used. Only the comparison, and the
numbers under it, say whether the estimate was right.

The rotation is applied twice, and the report should say so. Injecting a
skew and then removing it costs two interpolations where a real capture is
warped once, so the corrected image on screen is slightly softer than the
same page photographed straight would be, and any grid measurement from it
is pessimistic. The Python's roundtrip_control condition prices exactly
this and finds it costs almost nothing: PCC 0.9623 against 0.9624.

MEMORY, which is the risk this stage carries. On the 300 dpi fixture the
pass holds three Mats at once -- source 33.7 MB, injected-rotation 43.8 MB,
and the correction, which expands a second time, about 59 MB -- roughly
136 MB of native allocation, plus the 33.7 MB source bitmap on the Java
heap. Native memory is not heap-limited and every Mat is released in a
finally block, but this is the first point in the project where a 3 GB
device is plausibly under pressure, and it is the thing to watch for on
hardware.

NOT DONE, and previously implied. Re-running the grid estimate on the
CORRECTED image would be the natural on-device demonstration of CLAUDE.md
limitation 7 -- deskew must precede grid estimation -- and would retire the
"(pre-rotation)" tag added in 12.3. GridEstimator takes a Bitmap, so it
would mean materialising the full-resolution corrected image on the Java
heap, 43 MB on top of the 34 MB source. Left out deliberately rather than
risked on a device that is already the constraint. The tag stays and says
what it means.

NOT VERIFIED: all of it. Compile only.
